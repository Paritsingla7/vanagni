Next-day forest-fire risk for Uttarakhand

For every 5 km grid cell in Uttarakhand, this model predicts whether a satellite will detect a forest fire there tomorrow, from the cell's fire history, the weather, its fuel and terrain, and how close it is to people — roads, settlements, cropland. It is a backtest: every prediction is scored against what actually burned.

The question it tests: in a fire regime that is 90–95% human-caused, do human-proximity features add more predictive lift than weather? Confirmed or refuted, both are a real result.

Why this exists

India's Forest Survey runs a satellite alert system that tells state forest departments what is burning now. This asks the next question: which cells are most likely to burn tomorrow, so a limited inspection team can look in the right places first. Everything here is open data, and every claim is checked against the fire that followed, with leakage tests that fail loudly if any feature could see the future.

How to read the map

Scores are not calibrated probabilities, so cells are coloured by their rank within the day. The outlined cells are that day's top 20, a plausible daily inspection list; the map also reports how many of them burned.

Where this goes

BuiltUttarakhand 2017–2024: leakage-safe panel, staged ablation, an operational Fire Weather Index baseline, spatial-block holdout, this demo.
BuiltA second state is a config file, not new code: Odisha's grid, terrain, land cover, roads and districts are built by the same pipeline from config/odisha.yaml (its weather and modelling are not run yet).
NextOdisha weather and modelling (the download is a multi-day wait in Copernicus's queue) — the real test of whether the finding generalises.
NextSeasons that wrap the new year (Odisha's runs November to June), district-level human-ignition calendars, calibrated probabilities.
LaterLive daily scoring from a weather forecast instead of reanalysis, with a public scorecard of yesterday's hits.

What this is not

It is not an operational system or a safety tool. It is one state, a handful of seasons, and a research question; small differences between stages should be read with the caveats on the Ablation and Methodology pages.
